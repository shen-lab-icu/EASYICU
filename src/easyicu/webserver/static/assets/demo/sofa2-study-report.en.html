An experimental SOFA-2 Sepsis-3 phenotype and in-hospital mortality among adult ICU stays: a descriptive cohort analysis in MIMIC-IV

MIMIC-IV · run_20260815T061842_5049c6 · 2026-08-15

This page is not a manuscript. The run’s Writer withheld one under strict evidence enforcement; the page arranges the run’s registered tables and figures and adds no finding of its own.

Key results

  • Of 94,458 adult ICU stays, 33,997 (36.0%) met the experimental SOFA-2 sepsis phenotype within 24 hours of ICU admission.
  • In-hospital mortality was 13.2% (4,480 deaths) with the phenotype and 8.2% (4,986) without it; 10.0% overall.
  • Counts and proportions only: unadjusted, with no confidence intervals or hypothesis tests.

Methods

Data source

MIMIC-IV, a single-centre critical care database2, extracted with the EasyICU concept dictionary (concept-dict commit 190ab8f69, sofa2-dict commit 7f7ba49a2).

Study population

ICU stays of patients aged ≥ 18 years (94,458 stays). The unit of analysis is the ICU stay; the export carries no patient identifier, so repeat stays of one patient cannot be grouped.

Exposure

An experimental Sepsis-3 phenotype within hours 0–24 after ICU admission: a rise of ≥ 2 points in the SOFA-2 score around a suspected infection1. It substitutes SOFA-2 for the original SOFA and is a sensitivity definition, not the 2016 definition.

Outcome

In-hospital death, from admissions.hospital_expire_flag.

Analysis

Stays, share, deaths and in-hospital mortality by phenotype group, each over all stays in the group, reported following STROBE and RECORD3,4. The plan specified a descriptive analysis without confidence intervals, tests or effect estimates.

How far the result reads

  • Descriptive only. The difference in mortality between the groups is not an effect of the phenotype, and nothing is adjusted for confounding.
  • Exposure assessed after the start of follow-up. The phenotype is assessed over the first 24 hours, so stays ending in early death had a different opportunity to meet it5,6; the plan records this as an open limitation.
  • No patient identifier. Repeat stays of one patient count as separate stays, so no interval estimate is given.
