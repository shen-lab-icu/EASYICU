# Comparator review template

The skill computes an association; it does not establish novelty. When the
enclosing study asks "what does this add?", compare it on six prespecified
dimensions with **direct** comparators — studies of the same exposure family and
outcome in a comparable ICU population — rather than with the most recent or
most similar-sounding retrieval hit.

| Study (citation, PMID or DOI) | ① Population & setting | ② Exposure & time zero | ③ Outcome & estimand | ④ Analysis & robustness | ⑤ Data source & transportability | ⑥ Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| one row per screened direct comparator | design, size, setting | definition, classification window, time zero | outcome, effect scale, reported estimate | model, adjustment set, sensitivity analyses | registry, single centre or multinational | what it established |

**How to use the template**

1. Retrieve direct comparators first (same exposure family, same outcome,
   comparable ICU population); subgroup or special-population studies are
   context, not comparators.
2. Fill all six columns from the abstract or full text and cite each row; leave
   a cell as "not reported" rather than inferring it.
3. State in one sentence per column what the present study reuses and what it
   changes. A study whose changes are confined to ascertainment, time alignment,
   dependence handling or reporting is a methodological replication, not a new
   clinical finding; say so.
4. Compare effect sizes only across comparators with the same exposure window
   and a similar adjustment set. A different window or severity adjustment
   changes the estimand; report the difference instead of setting the numbers
   side by side.
5. Never write "novel" in the report generated by this skill; the claim ceiling
   is `analysis_only` and novelty is a separate, human-owned judgement.
