# Comparator table template: KDIGO/RIFLE stage and hospital mortality in ICU cohorts

The skill computes an association; it does not establish novelty. When the
enclosing study asks "what does this add?", fill the six prespecified dimensions
against **direct** comparators — studies of the same exposure family and outcome
in a general ICU population — rather than the most recent or most similar-sounding
retrieval hit. The rows below were assembled from PubMed abstracts on
2026-09-21 for the E3 study context (strict KDIGO stage at 0–24 h, in-hospital
death after a 24 h landmark, MIMIC-IV) and are kept here as the worked example;
a new study replaces them with its own comparators and cites PMIDs.

| Study | ① Population & setting | ② Exposure & time zero | ③ Outcome & estimand | ④ Analysis & robustness | ⑤ Data source & transportability | ⑥ Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hoste et al. 2015, *Intensive Care Med* (AKI-EPI; PMID 26162677) | 97 ICUs, 1,802 patients, prospective | KDIGO max stage in first ICU week (creatinine + urine output); t₀ = ICU admission | hospital mortality; adjusted OR stage 1 1.68 (0.89–3.17), stage 2 2.95 (1.38–6.28), stage 3 6.88 (3.88–12.23) | multivariable logistic ("adjusted for other variables") | multinational | first complete-KDIGO multinational epidemiology and stage–mortality gradient |
| Kellum et al. 2015, *JASN* (PMID 25568178) | UPMC, 32,045 ICU patients | KDIGO by creatinine and/or urine-output domain and duration | hospital mortality and RRT (4.3 %/0 % no AKI → 51.1 %/55.3 % both domains stage 3); 1-year survival | domain × stage cross-classification, survival | single academic centre | both domains and persistence carry the worst prognosis — supports separate creatinine/urine sensitivity axes |

**How to use the template**

1. Retrieve direct comparators first (same exposure family, same outcome, general
   ICU population); subgroup or special-population studies are context, not
   comparators.
2. Fill all six columns from the abstract or full text; leave a cell as "not
   reported" rather than inferring.
3. State in one sentence per column what the present study reuses and what it
   changes. In the E3 example the changes were confined to ② (strict two-domain
   ascertainment with an explicit unknown state; 24 h landmark), ④ (patient-level
   clustering; prespecified definition sensitivity) and ⑤ (denominators and
   baseline source made explicit) — a methodological replication, not a new
   clinical finding.
4. Never write "novel" in the report generated by this skill; the claim ceiling
   is `analysis_only` and novelty is a separate, human-owned judgement.
